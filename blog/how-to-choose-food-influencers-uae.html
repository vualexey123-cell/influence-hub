How to choose food influencers in the UAE: 7 criteria and common mistakes

12 July 2026 · 8 min read

Dubai is full of food accounts, and a big follower count tells you almost nothing about whether they'll fill your restaurant. Here are the seven things that actually predict results — and the traps that quietly burn budgets.

Picking the wrong creator isn't just a wasted post — it's a shoot your kitchen absorbed, a table you gave away, and reach that landed on people who'll never visit. Use these criteria to filter, in roughly this order of importance.

The 7 criteria

1. Where their audience actually is

This is the single most important factor for a physical venue. A creator with 200,000 followers spread across the GCC and beyond is worth less to your Dubai café than one with 8,000 followers who live in your neighbourhood. Ask for audience-location data and prioritise local reach over total reach.

2. Real engagement, not just likes

Look at comments, saves and shares — not the like count. Saves matter most for food: a save is someone bookmarking your place to visit. Genuine comments ("which branch is this?", "going this weekend") signal an audience that acts. A wall of emoji-only comments is a red flag.

3. Content quality and tone match

Does their style fit your venue? A high-energy fast-food reels creator and a calm specialty-coffee account attract different diners. The best match is a creator whose existing audience already looks like the guests you want.

4. Consistency

A creator who posts steadily has an audience that shows up steadily. Someone who posted daily last year and twice this month has a following that's drifting away — you'd be buying yesterday's reach.

5. Track record with venues

Have they featured restaurants before, and did those posts drive visible response? A creator who regularly turns meals into bookings is worth more than a bigger account that's never moved anyone off the couch.

6. Professionalism and reliability

Do they reply, show up, and post what was agreed, on time? In F&B this is operational, not just nice-to-have: a no-show wastes a reserved table and a prepped kitchen. Reliability is a real selection criterion.

7. Disclosure discipline

The UAE has clear rules on paid partnerships and advertising. A creator who labels partnerships correctly protects your brand; one who's sloppy about it creates risk. Professionalism here is a signal about everything else.

How to spot fake engagement

Watch for follower counts that dwarf the like and comment counts, sudden follower jumps, generic one-word comments, and engagement that doesn't match the account's size. In a market with as much bought reach as the UAE, a five-minute check saves you from an expensive dud.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing follower count. The vanity number that correlates least with walk-ins.
  • Ignoring audience location. Reach in Sharjah doesn't fill a table in Downtown Dubai.
  • Using the same three famous accounts as everyone else. Your target diners have banner-blindness for them.
  • One-and-done. A single post rarely changes anything; a steady rhythm of local creators does.
  • No agreement in writing. Fix deliverables, dates and disclosure before the visit — not after.
  • Skipping measurement. If you don't track saves, map clicks and bookings, you can't tell a good creator from a lucky one.

A simple shortlist process

  1. Filter by audience location first — local or nothing.
  2. Check engagement quality, especially saves and comments.
  3. Confirm tone and past venue results fit your place.
  4. Vet reliability and disclosure before you commit a table.
  5. Start with a small mix of local micro-creators, measure, then double down on what worked.
