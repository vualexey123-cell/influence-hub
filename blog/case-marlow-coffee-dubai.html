Marlow Coffee, Jumeirah: more new guests in one month than a full paid campaign

14 August 2026 · 7 min read · Dubai

A 40-seat specialty café in Jumeirah was busy on weekends and quiet the rest of the week. Instead of another round of ads, we sent nine local Dubai food creators through their doors across one month. Here is exactly what happened.

The situation

Marlow Coffee opened in Jumeirah in late 2025 with excellent beans, a short breakfast menu and a location just off the main strip — close enough to be reachable, far enough to be missed. Weekends filled up on word of mouth. Tuesday to Thursday, the room was half empty by 11am.

The team had tried boosted Instagram posts and a two-week paid campaign. Reach looked fine on paper, but the manager couldn't point to a single guest who had walked in because of it. What they wanted was simple: get the right people in the neighbourhood to actually taste the place and talk about it.

What we did

The whole engagement took about five minutes of the manager's time up front — a short brief about the venue, the kitchen, the dates creators could visit, and the terms. Everything after that was on us.

1. We built a Dubai-only line-up

No mega-influencers, no accounts from Abu Dhabi or overseas. We shortlisted creators whose audience actually lives and eats in Dubai, skewing toward Jumeirah, Umm Suqeim and Al Wasl. The manager crossed two names off the list — completely fine — and we replaced them the same week.

2. We spread visits across the month

The kitchen never faced five shoots on one Friday. Visits were scheduled two to four per week, weighted toward the quiet weekday mornings the café actually needed to fill. The host received the week's list in advance, so nobody was surprised at the pass.

3. We kept the tone human

Creators shot the food for their own audience in their own words. The paid-partnership label was there where UAE advertising rules require it, but nothing read like a brochure. The flat white, the kunafa croissant and the corner table did the talking.

The line-up that month

Nine creators, from a 6,400-follower Jumeirah brunch account to a 48,000-follower Dubai reels creator. Formats ranged from a single reel to a four-part story series. The mix mattered more than any single big name.

The results

Across the month the posts reached roughly 93,000 people in Dubai — not vanity impressions from a bought audience, but the feeds of locals who could realistically drive over on a Saturday. Three numbers stood out to the team:

  • Map clicks and saves. The café's Google and Instagram location saves more than doubled versus the previous month.
  • Weekday covers up 31%. The quiet Tuesday-to-Thursday mornings — the whole point of the campaign — started filling out.
  • A specific-dish effect. The kunafa croissant, featured by four of the nine creators, sold out three weekends in a row and became a permanent bestseller.
I used to spend a whole evening messaging people and end up with one story. Now I just see who's coming on Thursday and read one report at the end of the month. It brought more new faces than the ads did. — Layla, manager, Marlow Coffee, Jumeirah

What it cost

The venue was on our single-location plan: a fixed monthly subscription that already includes the creator arrangements, scheduling and one clear end-of-month report. No per-post negotiations, no surprise top-ups. For a café of this size, the month came in well below what a comparable paid-ads push had cost — and unlike ads, the nine posts keep working long after the campaign ended.

What's next

Marlow is now on a rolling monthly line-up: a fresh set of local creators every month rather than the same few faces, so the neighbourhood keeps discovering the place. The kunafa croissant, meanwhile, isn't going anywhere.
